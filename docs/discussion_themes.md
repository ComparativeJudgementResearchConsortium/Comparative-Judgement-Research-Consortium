## Overview

At the [October 2026 meeting](/meetings/2026-october.html) of the Comparative Judgement Research Consortium, participants took part in a Grand Challenges Activity led by Marie-Josée Bisson. It followed on from the [Grand Challenges 2026](/cj_rave.html) research agenda.

Participants split into four groups and each was given a small set of research questions and discussed:

- Which question interests you the most?
- What do you think is the next step to answer it?
- What evidence would you need to answer it?
- How would the answer to this question facilitate or advance your research?

Each participant then submitted their answers through an online form. The key themes below summarise those responses.

## Key themes

1. **Usability beats novelty.** Requests favour a regression-style toolbox, fast algorithms, and CJ engines that support new designs; NMM was flagged for not supporting the proposed pairing strategies.

2. **Multidimensionality and complexity.** This was the strongest pull, framed as a tension between realism and interpretability, and it raises the question of when item complexity makes CJ unviable.

3. **Validity, not just reliability.** There is a clear instinct to benchmark CJ against something external: absolute judgements, control groups in schools, disciplinary theory, model-fit diagnostics, and bias detection.

4. **Judges as the main source of uncertainty.** Heterogeneity, bias, and expertise versus crowdsourcing arise repeatedly. Prompts appear only obliquely, seen as assuming participant expertise while also restricting how participants think.

5. **Capability and collaboration gaps.** Several next steps are social rather than technical: read the literature, find a collaborator, talk to practitioners. The gap between applied statistics and practice is itself a barrier.

6. **A notable appetite for qualitative evidence.** Think-alouds, judge comments, student reactions to feedback, and perceptions of complexity are all in demand. This is unusual in a predominantly psychometric field, and a good hook for collaboration.
